# FAR/BAR Addenda, Disclosures and Related Forms

Read this when a FAR/BAR deal (the Standard or AS IS Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase) comes with any form other than the contract and its CR-7 riders: an addendum, counteroffer, extension, release, disclosure, acknowledgment, notice or worksheet. Each block says what the form changes, which dates and cancel rights it adds, and how it moves money, so a timeline, a seller's offer review or a buyer's offer can use it. The CR-7 riders are covered elsewhere; this file names them only where a form interacts with one.

## Reading These Forms

- **Precedence:** riders, addenda and typed or handwritten terms control printed contract terms in conflict with them (STANDARD R in both contracts). Most addenda here also say all non-conflicting terms stay in force.
- **Counting Days:** the contracts use calendar days; a period ending on a Saturday, Sunday or national legal holiday rolls to the next day that isn't one (STANDARD F). The roll does not apply to the time for acceptance or the Effective Date (Para. 3). Statutory condo and co-op rescission windows count 7 days excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays. EDRV-1 counts business days.
- **Which Contract:** record which contract the deal uses (AS IS or Standard) and every rider; the scripts apply that form's rules, so never work out a Standard or AS IS rule by hand. A form marked "Standard only" below never applies to an AS IS deal; don't use its terms there.
- **Deposit on a Cancel:** where a form gives a cancel right but doesn't say what happens to the Deposit, say so and document the refund with RC-4.
- **Defaults:** never invent one. Where the form prints no default for a blank, the block says "no default (ask)".

## Changing the Contract

### ACSP-4 Addendum to Contract

**Form:** `ACSP`, ACSP-4 Rev 6/17. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (also the CRSP).

- **Purpose:** a blank, numbered page for any added or changed terms, before or after the Effective Date.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Addendum No., the contract's Effective Date, Seller, Buyer, property, then free-text terms. No defaults (ask for each).
- **Deadlines:** only what the typed terms say. None printed.
- **Cancel Rights:** only what the typed terms say. None printed.
- **Money Effects:** only what the typed terms say (a price change, a seller credit, a repair credit are common).
- **Changes to the Contract:** whatever it states; it controls conflicting printed terms (STANDARD R). It binds only when signed by the parties to be bound (STANDARD P).
- **Red Flags:** missing signatures from any Buyer or Seller; an Effective Date that doesn't match the contract; terms that change a date without saying how it's counted; a repair or credit term on an AS IS deal that quietly reintroduces repair obligations; two addenda with the same number.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline re-runs every date the addendum moves and cites the addendum number. The seller's review reads each term as a change to price, credits, dates or contingencies and re-nets. The offer builder uses ACSP-4 for terms no printed form covers, and prefers EA-4 for extensions and CO-3 for counters.

### CO-3 Counter Offer

**Form:** `CO`, CO-3 Rev 1/15. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (any sale contract).

- **Purpose:** answers an offer by changing selected terms; once signed by all and delivered, the initial offer as amended by this counter becomes the contract.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Counter Offer number; parties; date of the initial offer; property. Checkboxes: Purchase Price changed to $; Closing Date changed to; items included; items excluded; Other, as line-numbered changes to the offer. Acceptance deadline: date, or **within 2 days after the counter offer is delivered** if left blank. No other defaults.
- **Deadlines:** by the stated date (default 2 days after delivery): the receiving party signs and an executed copy is delivered to all parties. If missed: the counter is deemed withdrawn and any deposit is returned to Buyer. Safe reading: the weekend roll of STANDARD F doesn't extend this deadline, because acceptance times are carved out of it.
- **Cancel Rights:** none; before acceptance the counter simply lapses or is withdrawn.
- **Money Effects:** a price change moves the balance to close; a Loan Amount stated as a percentage moves with it, one stated in dollars doesn't (check the buyer can still close). Deposits don't change unless a line changes them.
- **Changes to the Contract:** only the checked items and listed lines change. "All terms and conditions of the initial offer remain the same" means the original offer's printed terms, riders and blanks stand as written. It does **not** carry forward terms from an earlier counter unless they are restated in this one. The Effective Date is when the last party signs and delivers the final counter (Para. 3(b)), so every "days after Effective Date" period starts from that date.
- **Red Flags:** a counter that relies on an earlier counter's terms without restating them; line numbers that don't match the offer's line numbers; a changed Closing Date with no look at the dates counted back from it (Title Evidence Deadline, survey, permit close-out, rider F's appraisal date); a price change that breaks an escalation, appraisal gap or FHA/VA rider amount; counters sent to more than one buyer with no written reservation to be bound by only one (CO-3 prints no such clause, see MODS-1).
- **Counter on the Contract:** a seller can also counter without CO-3, by checking "Seller counters Buyer's offer" (AS IS line 613, Standard line 699), striking offered terms and writing new ones, each initialed. Read the written-in value, not the struck one. The buyer accepts by initialing every change; the Effective Date is the last of those initials once delivered (Para. 3(b)). The counter's acceptance time is Para. 3(a)'s for counters (2 days after delivery unless stated), not the buyer's original offer deadline. A price change on the contract should also change the Loan Amount (if in dollars) and the balance to close; if they weren't changed, the money no longer adds up (see `farbar-package-check.md`, Consistency).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline sets the Effective Date from the final counter and applies each changed term; it reads all counters in order and keeps only terms restated in the last one. The seller's review models each counter as a new net and certainty score. The offer builder drafts counters as CO-3 lines tied to the offer's line numbers and sets an acceptance date instead of relying on the 2-day default when timing matters.

### EA-4 Extension Addendum to Contract

**Form:** `EA`, EA-4 Rev 3/21. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (also CRSP, Vacant Land and Commercial).

- **Purpose:** extends one or more of the contract's dates or periods on otherwise unchanged terms.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** contract Effective Date, parties, property, then check the periods that apply:
  - **Closing Date:** extended **until a stated date** (no "days" option).
  - **Financing Period:** "Commitment Period, Loan Approval Date, or Financing Period" extended for an additional N days **or** until a date. On FAR/BAR this is the Para. 8(b) Loan Approval Period.
  - **Inspection Period:** additional N days or until a date (Para. 12(a)).
  - **Title Cure Period:** "Curative Period or Cure Period", additional N days or until a date (STANDARD A's 30-day Cure Period).
  - **Short Sale Approval Deadline:** rider G.
  - **Sale/Lease of Buyer's Property Deadline:** rider V.
  - **Due Diligence Period:** Vacant Land and Commercial contracts only; it doesn't exist in FAR/BAR.
  - An "except" section for changed terms. No defaults (ask for each checked period).
- **Deadlines:** the new date for each checked period. Safe reading for "additional N days": add N to the period's own day count (a 10-day Inspection Period extended 5 days becomes 15 days after the Effective Date), then apply the weekend and holiday rule once to the new end. Counting from an end that had already rolled past a weekend gives a later date, so it isn't the safe reading; mention it to the agent when the two differ. When the form gives a date, use the date. When both are filled and disagree, flag it and ask.
- **Cancel Rights:** none of its own; each extended period's existing right now ends on the new date.
- **Money Effects:** none printed; money terms can appear in the "except" lines (for example a per diem or an extra deposit).
- **Changes to the Contract:** only the checked dates, plus any "except" terms. Dates the contract counts back from Closing Date (Title Evidence Deadline, 5-day survey, 5-day permit close-out, walk-through, rider F default appraisal date) move with a new Closing Date. The Standard contract's repair steps (Seller's 10 days, the 5-day election) run from their own triggers, so an Inspection Period extension moves only Buyer's notice deadline. After a Loan Approval Period extension, Seller's 3-day termination window in Para. 8(b)(v) runs from the new end. EA-4 has no box for deposits, rider F's appraisal date, association approval or a condo or co-op document deadline: put those in the "except" lines or use ACSP-4.
- **Red Flags:** signed after the period already expired (a lapsed right may not revive; flag it for the agent); only one party signed; the "Financing" box used when the lender needs a new commitment date that the contract doesn't use; a Closing Date extension that runs past a rate lock, a CFPB 7-day extension already used (Para. 5(a)), or a lease-back or occupancy date.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline replaces each checked date, re-derives every date counted from Closing Date, and lists the unchanged ones so nothing is silently carried. The seller's review weighs an extension request as a certainty signal (why it's needed, what the buyer gives). The offer builder uses EA-4 for date changes after the Effective Date and states new periods as dates.

### RC-4 Release and Cancellation of Contract

**Form:** `RC`, RC-4 Rev. 3/19. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (checkboxes for CRSP, AS IS FAR/BAR, FAR/BAR, Commercial, Vacant Land).

- **Purpose:** the parties cancel the contract, release each other and both brokerages, and tell the escrow agent how to pay out the deposit.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Buyer; Seller; which contract (check one); Effective Date; property. Para. 2: the two brokerages released. Para. 3: up to four lines of "$ amount to payee". No defaults (ask; the amounts must be agreed).
- **Deadlines:** None. The escrow agent disburses after it has the release signed by all Buyers and Sellers.
- **Cancel Rights:** it is the cancellation. Signing ends the contract whether or not a contingency was the reason.
- **Money Effects:** splits the Deposit as written in Para. 3. If one party won't sign, the agent keeps holding the Deposit under Para. 13 (interpleader, or FREC procedures when a broker holds it).
- **Changes to the Contract:** ends it. The mutual release covers all claims arising from or relating to the contract or pre-contract issues existing on the release date, including against both brokerages.
- **Red Flags:** Para. 3 amounts that don't add up to the Deposit actually collected (check EDRV-1 or the escrow letter); a deposit paid to Seller when a contingency entitled Buyer to a refund, or the reverse; the wrong contract box; a missing co-owner signature; a party signing away a claim (for example over a disclosure) without understanding the release.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline closes the file and records the release date and deposit split. The seller's review treats a signed RC-4 on a prior contract as the listing being free to accept (a backup under rider W moves up). The offer builder doesn't use it.

### EAC-1 Escalation Addendum to Contract

**Form:** `EAC`, © 2021 (EAC-1). **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (checkboxes for AS IS FAR/BAR, FAR/BAR, CRSP, Commercial, Vacant Land).

- **Purpose:** the buyer's price rises to beat a bona fide competing offer by a set amount, up to a cap.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** base Purchase Price the buyer offered; contract (check one); Escalation Amount ($ over the competing offer's price); Maximum Purchase Price; how the escalation is paid: (a) cash at closing with proof of funds attached, or (b) financed. **If neither box is checked, (a) cash applies.** Seller fills in the revised Purchase Price. No other defaults (ask for the increment and the cap).
- **Deadlines:** at or before the time Seller accepts or counters in writing, Seller provides a copy of the Competing Offer with the competing buyer's identity redacted. If missed: the price does not increase.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** revised price = competing offer's price + Escalation Amount, capped at the Maximum Purchase Price; when the sum passes the cap, the price is the cap. Qualifying competing offer: bona fide, unexpired (still within its time for acceptance), received by and acceptable to Seller. Under (a) the extra amount is buyer cash; under (b) the financed amount is amended to include it. Safe reading: the price never drops below the base offer (the form only "increases" it). Ambiguity: the form calls the payment "the applicable Escalation Amount", which it defines as the increment over the competing offer, while the actual increase is revised price minus base price; plan the buyer's cash for the full increase.
- **Changes to the Contract:** replaces Para. 2's price with the revised price Seller writes in; under (b) it also amends the Loan Amount.
- **Red Flags:** no Competing Offer copy delivered (no escalation); a competing offer that is not bona fide, expired, or not acceptable to Seller; comparing price only when the competing offer has a seller credit, a different closing cost split or its own escalation (the form is silent; flag and ask); under (a) no proof of funds attached; under (b) a lender who won't finance above appraised value. The form does not address appraisal: an escalated price that appraises low falls under Para. 8(b) (lender's Appraisal is part of Loan Approval), rider F if attached (its default value is the Purchase Price, now the escalated price), rider E for FHA/VA, or AGA-1 if attached. The acknowledgments note the addendum reveals the buyer's top price and lets Seller share the offer.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline uses the revised price. The seller's review verifies the Competing Offer is bona fide and unexpired, computes the escalated price (cap included), checks that the escalated amount is funded (cash proof or lender), and runs the appraisal-risk scenario at the escalated price. The offer builder sets the increment and cap inside the buyer's limits, pairs a high cap with appraisal protection or an appraisal gap the buyer can fund, and shows buyer cash at the cap.

### AGA-1 Appraisal Gap Addendum

**Form:** `AGA`, AGA-1 Rev 9/22. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts, conventional or cash only. The form says not to use it with the FAR/BAR Appraisal Contingency Rider (CR-7 F) or the CRSP appraisal addendum; it is not for FHA or VA loans (rider E's appraisal clause conflicts).

- **Purpose:** the buyer agrees to cover a low appraisal or other valuation up to a set Gap Amount.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Gap Amount (max $, no default: ask); how paid: (a) cash with proof of funds attached, or (b) financed; **(a) if neither is checked**. Valuation deadline: N days after Effective Date, **30 if blank**. Renegotiation window: N days after Buyer delivers the Valuation, **3 if blank**.
- **Deadlines:**
  - 30 days (default) after Effective Date: Buyer obtains the Valuation (through the lender, or on Buyer's own if no lender). If missed: the form is silent; flag it.
  - 3 days after obtaining it: Buyer delivers a copy to Seller. If missed: the form is silent; flag it.
  - 3 days after Buyer delivers the Valuation: the parties sign an addendum setting the new terms (price, financed amount, cash to close).
  - If Valuation + Gap Amount is still below the price: 3 days (default) after delivery to agree revised terms in a signed addendum. If missed: the contract terminates and Buyer's deposit is returned.
- **Cancel Rights:** automatic termination with deposit refund when the shortfall exceeds the Gap Amount and no revised-terms addendum is signed in time. Seller is never obligated to reduce the price.
- **Money Effects:** Buyer pays (Purchase Price − Valuation), up to the Gap Amount, never more than the price. Under (a) this is extra buyer cash; lenders usually size the loan on the lower value, so cash to close also rises by the down payment difference. Under (b) the financed amount is amended to include the gap (the buyer must confirm the lender allows it). Ambiguity: the form speaks of an addendum "setting forth the new Purchase Price" even when the gap is covered; safe reading is that the price stays and the addendum restates the loan amount and cash to close.
- **Changes to the Contract:** adds a valuation step and a termination that works like a capped appraisal contingency. It does not remove the Para. 8(b) financing contingency: a lender who won't approve at the low value still gives the buyer the 8(b)(iv) out, if the buyer acts in the Loan Approval Period.
- **Red Flags:** attached together with rider F, or with rider E (FHA/VA): conflicting terms, ask which governs; Gap Amount larger than the buyer's verified cash under (a); no proof of funds; option (b) with no lender confirmation; a Valuation deadline after the Loan Approval Period ends.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds the Valuation deadline, the 3-day delivery, and the 3-day renegotiation window with its automatic termination. The seller's review computes the guaranteed floor (price minus Gap Amount) and whether the buyer's cash covers it. The offer builder sizes the Gap Amount from the buyer's reserve, never with FHA/VA or rider F, and shows cash to close at the full gap.

### BBCCA-1 Buyer's Broker Compensation Contingency Addendum

**Form:** `BBCCA`, BBCCA-1 Rev. 9/2024. **Used With:** names the FAR/BAR "Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase" and the CRSP. Its text is identical to CR-7 rider GG, which is printed for both FAR/BAR contracts; on FAR/BAR prefer rider GG, and never attach both.

- **Purpose:** makes the contract contingent on a compensation agreement for the buyer's broker (usually CASSB-1).
- **Blanks and Defaults:** who signs the compensation agreement with the Buyer's Broker: Seller's Broker, or Seller (check one; no default: ask). Time Period: N days after Effective Date, **3 if blank**. The agreement's terms must be acceptable to Buyer.
- **Deadlines:** 3 days (default) after Effective Date: the compensation agreement is executed and delivered. If missed: Buyer may cancel within the next 3 days by written notice. If Buyer doesn't: the contingency ends and the contract continues.
- **Cancel Rights:** Buyer only, in the 3-day window after the Time Period; Deposit refunded.
- **Money Effects:** none in the addendum itself; the money is in the compensation agreement. If Seller is the payer, the fee comes off the seller's net. If Seller's Broker is the payer, whether it reduces the seller's net depends on the listing agreement: don't assume. The buyer's exposure is whatever the buyer brokerage agreement leaves unpaid.
- **Changes to the Contract:** adds a buyer contingency early in the deal; nothing else.
- **Red Flags:** no party box checked; the compensation agreement not signed in time and the 3-day notice window unnoticed; both BBCCA-1 and rider GG attached; an agreement amount above the buyer brokerage agreement (CASSB-1 caps it there).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds the Time Period deadline and the Buyer's 3-day cancel window. The seller's review lists the compensation as a cost only when Seller is the payer, shows its effect on net, and scores the contingency as short-lived. The offer builder uses it (or rider GG) when the buyer's broker compensation isn't already agreed, and compares it with a seller credit under rider FF.

### CASSB-1 Compensation Agreement: Seller or Seller's Broker to Buyer's Broker

**Form:** `CASSB`, CASSB-1 Rev 1/26. **Used With:** any contract; it is a separate agreement between brokers (or seller and broker), not an addendum. The buyer does not sign it.

- **Purpose:** Seller or Seller's Broker agrees to pay the Buyer's Broker at closing when that broker is the procuring cause.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Para. 1 payer: Seller or Seller's Broker (check one). Para. 2 property; if Seller pays, whether it is listed (name the listing broker) or not listed. Para. 3 buyer name (optional). Para. 4 Term: N days, **15 if blank**. Para. 5 amount: flat fee, or a percent of the gross purchase price plus $, or other. Para. 6 dispute resolution, with optional arbitration initials. No default amount (ask).
- **Deadlines:** the Term starts when fully executed and delivered and never runs past the listing's end (including extensions and protection periods). Once a contract with a buyer the Buyer's Broker procured is fully executed, the Term extends through that contract's actual closing.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** paid at closing if the (named, or any) buyer closes and the Buyer's Broker is procuring cause during the Term. Never more than the amount in the Buyer's Broker's written agreement with the buyer (Para. 7). If Seller pays, it reduces the seller's net; if Seller's Broker pays, check the listing agreement for whether it comes from the listing commission.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none directly; it is what satisfies BBCCA-1 or rider GG.
- **Red Flags:** a Term that expires before the contract is signed; a named buyer different from the contract's buyer; payer box unchecked; an amount higher than the buyer brokerage agreement; any mention of sharing the offer in MLS fields (the form says not to).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline notes the agreement date against BBCCA-1 or GG. The seller's review adds the fee as a seller cost when Seller is the payer and checks it against any rider FF credit so compensation isn't paid twice. The offer builder confirms the agreement exists before relying on seller-paid compensation.

### CDDA-2 Community Development District Addendum

**Form:** `CDDA`, CDDA 2 Rev 2/18. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (also Vacant Land).

- **Purpose:** the Chapter 190 disclosure that a Community Development District may levy taxes and assessments on the property, plus who pays them. The form says it's required for the initial sale of a parcel or unit after the district is established, not later sales; it is still used on resales to allocate the charges.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** district name; parties; property; Seller's representation of current CDD taxes and assessments on up to two lines ($ per period, payable to). No defaults (ask).
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** Buyer takes all district assessments and charges, **including any outstanding capital assessment** (remaining bond principal stays with the property). Seller pays annual assessments for years before the closing year at or before closing. The closing year's annual and capital assessments are prorated like property taxes (Standard and AS IS STANDARD K also prorate CDD charges; Para. 9(f) excludes them from the special assessment rule).
- **Changes to the Contract:** adds the disclosure and this allocation; overrides any conflicting special-assessment treatment.
- **Red Flags:** blank amount lines (the representation sits below the signature lines, so check it was filled); a large outstanding capital assessment not reflected in the buyer's payment; delinquent prior-year CDD charges; the CDD shown on the tax bill as a non-ad valorem line that the seller's taxes quote left out.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline has no dates to add. The seller's review confirms prior-year charges are paid and the closing-year proration. The offer builder adds the annual CDD charge to the monthly payment and states any capital balance the buyer inherits.

### COOP-4 Addendum to Contract for Sale and Purchase of Cooperative

**Form:** `COOP`, COOP-4 Rev 7/25. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts when the property is a co-op. No CR-7 rider covers co-ops (rider A is condo only). It supersedes inconsistent contract terms; terms are read with s. 719.103 definitions. The seller fills it out.

- **Purpose:** converts the contract to the sale of co-op shares and a proprietary lease, and carries the s. 719.503 disclosures and rescission rights.
- **Blanks and Defaults:**
  - **A:** apartment number and building name.
  - **B:** blanket mortgage: is / is not (no default: ask); Seller's share of principal.
  - **C:** ground lease: wholly owned with no ground lease, or held under a ground lease with Buyer paying a share of rent and expenses, or expenses only (ask).
  - **H(1):** date documents were received, if before the contract; the $ escrow may pay Seller for unreturned documents.
  - **H(2):** Buyer applies for association approval within N days (**no default printed: ask**).
  - **H(5):** parking, boat slip and storage numbers.
  - **H(6):** who pays installments of a special assessment imposed after the Effective Date: Buyer or Seller, **Buyer if blank**. Also Seller's known pending assessments, litigation, current fees (up to 3 lines), and recreation or land lease (is / is not, monthly amount).
  - **H(7):** common-element casualty cap: $ or %, **1.5% if blank**.
  - **H(9):** check one of the two s. 719.503 statements.
  - **H(10)(a)–(c):** milestone summary, SIRS and turnover report status, each option (i), (ii) or (iii).
  - **H(10)(d):** one of two statements when any (i) is checked.
- **Deadlines:**
  - 10 days after Effective Date: Seller furnishes the lender's statement of Seller's blanket-mortgage share (B).
  - N days after Effective Date: Buyer applies for association approval and pursues it diligently (H(2)); the form sets no date by which approval must come, so the Closing Date governs.
  - 3 days after the association's right-of-first-refusal decision: Seller gives Buyer written notice (H(3)).
  - Within STANDARD A's title examination time (5 days after receiving the title commitment): Buyer may cancel if a ground lease has under 50 years left (C).
  - No more than 10 days before Closing Date: Seller's UCC search of Florida Secretary of State records (E).
  - 5 days before Closing Date: the association must have set any casualty assessment, or either party may cancel (H(7)(b)).
  - Before closing: Seller delivers the milestone summary, SIRS (or a statement none is done) and turnover report when they apply (H(10)), and the association's notice if owners voted to forego sprinkler retrofitting (H(8)).
  - Rescission: 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after the later of Buyer signing and Buyer receiving the documents (H(9): articles, bylaws, rules and the question and answer sheet; H(10)(d)(ii): milestone summary, turnover report and SIRS). Buyer may extend closing up to 7 such days after receipt. The rights end at closing and can't be waived.
- **Cancel Rights:**
  - Buyer, if Seller's blanket-mortgage share exceeds the price (B; no time limit stated).
  - Buyer, for a ground lease under 50 years, within the title examination time (C).
  - Automatic termination with Deposit refund if the association rejects the Buyer (H(2)), or exercises its right of first refusal (H(3)), "unless this Contract provides otherwise".
  - Either party, with Deposit refund, after common-element casualty (H(7)): the unit appraises below price and no new price is agreed or Buyer won't proceed; the assessment isn't set 5 days before closing; or it exceeds the cap.
  - Buyer's statutory rescission under H(9) and H(10)(d)(ii), when the "voidable" statement is checked.
- **Money Effects:**
  - Seller's blanket-mortgage share is **not included** in the price, so the buyer's true cost is price plus the share assumed.
  - **Seller pays:** transfer and assumption document preparation, recording of Seller's existing co-op documents, documentary stamps on the transfer documents, estoppel fees, fines, association information fees, and dues current to closing.
  - **Buyer pays:** recording of transfer and assumption documents, recording of the transfer and membership approval, association resale or transfer and application fees, and the leasehold title policy.
  - Special assessments imposed after the Effective Date: Seller pays what's due before closing, Buyer what's due after, or Seller pays in full if checked.
  - If the right of first refusal is exercised, Seller still pays the broker's full commission.
  - Unreturned documents can cost Buyer the listed $ from the deposit.
- **Changes to the Contract:** title insurance becomes leasehold title insurance in the amount of the price (G). Seller's warranty and risk of loss cover only the unit and its limited common elements (H). Closing documents add share certificates, stock powers, the proprietary lease and its assignment, association consent and an estoppel letter (E).
- **Red Flags:** H(2) approval days blank; neither H(9) box checked, or both; the "acknowledges receipt" box checked without documents actually received more than 7 business days before signing; H(10) any (i) checked with no (d) choice; a ground lease near 50 years; a blanket mortgage share with no lender statement. Form quirks: page 1's footer says "Page 1 of 3" while the others say "of 5"; H(10)(b)(i) and (c)(i) point to a Paragraph 10(e) that doesn't exist (the rescission text is 10(d)). Read them as 10(d).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds the 10-day lender statement, the approval application date, the UCC search window, the 5-day casualty deadline, and each rescission window from the receipt dates on RCD-8 (business days), marking the deal not firm until they pass. The seller's review subtracts the seller-paid co-op costs, flags approval and first-refusal risk in certainty, and notes Seller's assessment choice. The offer builder adds the blanket-mortgage share and maintenance to the buyer's real cost, checks lender acceptance of co-op financing, and fills the approval days.

### AA-1 Auction Addendum to Purchase and Sale Contract

**Form:** `AA`, AA-1 Rev. 12/2010. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (the form names the "Residential Sale and Purchase Contract").

- **Purpose:** adapts the contract to a sale by auction.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Buyer Premium as % of the high bid (no default: ask). Cost checkboxes, each Buyer / Seller / N/A: deed recording, mortgage satisfaction and recording, documentary stamps on the deed, owner's title policy, title search, tax search. No defaults; safe reading if a line is blank: the contract's Para. 9 allocation applies.
- **Deadlines:** 5 days after Effective Date: Seller furnishes any home inspection report Seller has. If missed: no remedy stated.
- **Cancel Rights:** None; Buyer has no right to cancel over inspection results, and no financing contingency.
- **Money Effects:** contract price = high bid + Buyer Premium (the premium goes to the auctioneer). Cash purchase. Closing costs follow the checkboxes.
- **Changes to the Contract:** Para. 8 becomes cash with no financing contingency. It **deletes the inspection, repair and walk-through provisions**: on the Standard form that removes Para. 12 and makes the Para. 9(a) repair limits moot; on the AS IS form it removes the Para. 12(a) walk-away right. The property is taken as is, with only a visual inspection before the auction unless agreed otherwise. Seller still maintains the property in the same condition, except normal wear and tear.
- **Red Flags:** a buyer who needs a loan; a Standard contract still showing repair limits; blank cost boxes; a buyer who expects a walk-through (it's deleted); premium not counted in the buyer's cash.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline drops the Inspection Period, repair steps, Loan Approval Period and walk-through, and adds the 5-day report delivery. The seller's review scores it as cash with no inspection or financing out and nets on the high bid (the premium isn't Seller's). The offer builder sets the bid ceiling so bid + premium + costs stay inside the buyer's cash.

### SPRA-2 Septic Program Replacement Addendum

**Form:** `SPRA`, SPRA-2 Rev 3/2024. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts.

- **Purpose:** gives Buyer time to look into a county septic-to-sewer replacement program and cancel if the facts are unacceptable.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Septic Replacement Inspection Period: N days from Effective Date (**no default printed: ask**).
- **Deadlines:** within N days from Effective Date: Buyer's inquiry (at Buyer's expense) with the utility, BMAP, water management district or other authority into availability, schedule, assessments, capacity fees, hookup, decommissioning, credits and incentives. 3 days after that period ends: Buyer's written cancel notice. If missed: Buyer accepts all septic-program conditions and can't cancel under this addendum.
- **Cancel Rights:** Buyer, in Buyer's sole discretion on the listed topics, by written notice no later than 3 days after the period ends. The form doesn't say the Deposit is refunded; safe reading: document the refund with RC-4.
- **Money Effects:** inquiry costs are Buyer's. Program assessments, capacity and connection fees are what the inquiry is for; the addendum doesn't allocate them (Para. 9(f) governs public-body assessments).
- **Changes to the Contract:** adds this inquiry period and cancel right.
- **Red Flags:** blank period; a cancel window that closes after the Inspection Period, so the buyer thinks the inspection deadline also covers it; the addendum used without MDSTS-2 on a Miami-Dade septic property.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds the inquiry period end and the cancel deadline 3 days later. The seller's review counts it as an open buyer walk-away until that date. The offer builder fills the days and pairs it with a septic inspection inside the Inspection Period.

## Inspection and Walk-Through

### BRR-1 Buyer's Request for Repairs and/or Remedies

**Form:** `BRR`, © 2014 (BRR-1). **Used With:** Standard only (Para. 12). It does not fit the AS IS contract, where Seller owes no repairs: on AS IS a repair ask is a negotiation written as an ACSP-4 addendum, and Buyer's leverage is the Para. 12(a) walk-away before the Inspection Period ends.

- **Purpose:** Buyer's written notice, within the Inspection Period, of items not in the condition the contract requires. It requests the remedies the contract provides and does not amend the contract.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** property; General Repair and warranted items; WDO items; Permit Inspection items; for each report: attached, or will be forwarded separately. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:**
  - **Buyer:** deliver within the Inspection Period (Para. 12(a), 15 days after Effective Date if blank), with the WDO report if WDO evidence is found and, on Seller's written request, the relevant portion of the General inspector's report. If missed: Buyer waives Seller's repair obligations for items not timely reported (Seller's Maintenance Requirement continues).
  - **General Items (Para. 12(b)(iii)):** 10 days after receiving the notice or report, Seller completes the repairs, or gets a licensed estimate, or gets a second inspection with estimates. If the reports differ, the parties split the cost of a third inspector whose report binds. At or under the General Repair Limit, Seller repairs. Over the limit: within 5 days after a party receives the last estimate, Seller may elect to pay the excess, or Buyer may designate repairs up to the limit and take the rest as is. If neither acts, either party may cancel.
  - **WDO Items (Para. 12(c)(ii)):** 10 days after receiving the WDO report, Seller gets damage and treatment estimates. Up to the WDO Repair Limit, Seller treats and repairs; no retreatment is needed if the same WDO was treated before, there's no live infestation, and Seller transfers a full treatment warranty. Over the limit: within 5 days after receiving Seller's estimate, Buyer agrees to pay the excess or designates repairs up to the limit. Seller has no election here. If Buyer doesn't act, either party may cancel.
  - **Permit Items (Para. 12(d)(ii)):** 10 days after receiving the notice, Seller gets a cost estimate. No later than 5 days before Closing Date, Seller closes the permits up to the Permit Limit. Over the limit: within 5 days after receiving the estimates, Seller may pay the excess, or Buyer may accept as is with a credit equal to the Permit Limit. If neither acts, either party may cancel. A government delay in final inspections extends closing up to 10 days; after that either party may cancel.
- **Cancel Rights:** either party, with Deposit refunded, only in the over-the-limit cases above when no election is made, and after a 10-day permit delay. The notice itself creates no walk-away.
- **Money Effects:** Seller pays up to each limit in Para. 9(a): General Repair, WDO Repair and Permit, each **1.5% of the price if blank**. Unfinished work at closing is escrowed at 125% of the estimated cost, capped at the limits; unused escrow returns to Seller. Buyer may pay excess to keep the deal.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none; it triggers the Para. 12 process. Only items in the Para. 12(b)(ii) condition standard qualify: leaks, water or structural damage, and working condition, plus torn screens, fogged windows and missing roof tiles or shingles. Cosmetic Conditions don't qualify, and neither do cracked tiles, worn shingles or limited roof life without leaks.
- **Red Flags:** delivered after the Inspection Period; cosmetic items listed as repairs; no report attached when Seller asked; a BRR-1 used on an AS IS contract; limits left blank without noticing the 1.5% defaults; missed 5-day election windows (either party can then cancel).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds the Seller's 10-day response, the 5-day election window after the last estimate, the 5-days-before-closing permit deadline and the possible 10-day permit extension, each with its cancel consequence. The seller's review measures repair exposure against each limit and nets it. The offer builder sets the three limits deliberately (they're the buyer's protection on Standard) and never plans a BRR-1 on AS IS.

### BWTIR-1 Buyer's Walk-Through Inspection/Reinspection

**Form:** `BWTIR`, © 2012 (BWTIR-1). **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts (Standard Para. 12(e); AS IS Para. 12(b)).

- **Purpose:** records the walk-through or a reinspection, confirming condition and personal property before closing, and releases the licensee.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** property; walk-through or reinspection (check one); access provided or not; utilities provided or not; exceptions (what isn't done). No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** the walk-through is the day before Closing Date or on Closing Date before closing, as Buyer specifies (contract). None on the form.
- **Cancel Rights:** None. Exceptions are handled by the contract: Standard Para. 9(a) escrow of 125% of estimated cost (capped at the limits), or default remedies.
- **Money Effects:** none on the form; listed exceptions can lead to an escrow holdback or a negotiated credit.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. Buyer acknowledges that, except as listed, required repairs are done, the Maintenance Requirement is met (the property is in substantially the same condition as on the Effective Date, except normal wear and tear), included personal property is present, and other obligations are met. Buyer releases the licensee and brokerage. Seller signs only for receipt.
- **Red Flags:** signing with no exceptions when repairs are incomplete (it's an acknowledgment); utilities off (systems can't be tested); on AS IS, the repairs line doesn't apply unless an addendum required repairs; the form uses the Standard's term "Maintenance Requirement", which is the "AS IS Maintenance Requirement" on the AS IS form.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline places the walk-through on the day before or the day of closing. The seller's review has no use before acceptance. The offer builder notes that the walk-through is a check, not a cancel right.

## Seller Disclosures

### `SPDR-4x` Seller's Property Disclosure: Residential

**Form:** `SPDR`, SPDR-4x Rev 3/25. **Used With:** any contract (houses, townhouses; condos use SPDC-2). It is not part of the contract.

- **Purpose:** the seller's written disclosure of known facts that materially affect value and aren't readily observable (Johnson v. Davis), from the seller's actual knowledge.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** occupancy (owner, tenant, unoccupied and for how long); Yes / No / Don't Know items with explanations:
  1. Structures, systems and appliances (aluminum wiring, leased appliances).
  2. Termites, other WDO and pests.
  3. Water intrusion, drainage and flooding, special flood hazard area, seaward of the CCCL, lender-required flood insurance, elevation certificate.
  4. Plumbing (water source, treatment system, sewer or septic location, unused tanks or wells, leaks, polybutylene).
  5. Roof (condition, age or install date, leaks, work done, defects).
  6. Pool safety features (s. 515.27) and demolished or filled pools.
  7. Sinkholes and sinkhole claims.
  8. HOA and restrictions, shared features, encroachments, boundary disputes, severed subsurface rights (s. 689.29), private or public roads.
  9. Environmental (pre-1978, hazards, mangroves).
  10. Government, claims and litigation (special assessments, s. 163.081 Qualifying Improvements, insurance claims, zoning, flood elevation, unpermitted work, open permits, code violations, historic district).
  11. FIRPTA.
  12. Additional comments addendum.

  No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** Seller promises to notify Buyer promptly in writing if anything becomes inaccurate (use SPDU-1). None else.
- **Cancel Rights:** none from the form. Answers feed rights elsewhere: the AS IS Para. 12(a) walk-away, Standard Para. 12 and 10(d) (flood zone and elevation: Buyer may cancel within 20 days after Effective Date if blank).
- **Money Effects:** none directly; answers point to cost (roof age, polybutylene, open permits, PACE-type assessments).
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. It supports the seller's representation in Para. 10(j).
- **Red Flags:** "Don't Know" on items the seller should know (roof age, septic); yes answers without explanation; open permits (Standard 12(d) and the Permit Limit); a paid sinkhole claim (use SD-2); seaward of the CCCL (CCCLA-3, rider N); pre-1978 (rider P, LBPL-1x); a Qualifying Improvements assessment (rider EE); mandatory HOA (rider B); FIRPTA yes (withholding at closing); unsigned by the buyer.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline has no dates. The seller's review checks it was given and signed, and lists yes answers as risks to certainty. The offer builder turns yes answers and unknowns into inspection focus, cost estimates and questions for the listing agent.

### SPDC-2 Seller's Property Disclosure: Condominium

**Form:** `SPDC`, SPDC-2 Rev. 10/24. **Used With:** any contract, for condo units (in place of SPDR-4x). It is not part of the contract.

- **Purpose:** the condo seller's disclosure. Items 1 to 5 cover only the unit; items 6 to 9 cover limited common elements, the association, structural studies and FIRPTA.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** occupancy; Yes / No / Don't Know items with explanations:
  1. Structures and systems (whether the roof and HVAC are association-maintained, added structural reinforcement).
  2. WDO.
  3. Water intrusion, polybutylene, leaks to or from adjoining units, lender flood insurance.
  4. Fire sprinklers and any association vote to forego retrofitting, unapproved or unpermitted alterations, base flood elevation, open permits.
  5. Hazardous substances.
  6. Exclusive-use limited common elements (parking, storage, slips) and the document granting them.
  7. The association: proposed changes to documents or common elements, litigation, construction-defect claims, discussion of converting the condominium, an investor or group buying units, approved but not yet implemented fee increases, flood zone, CCCL, sinkholes, structural damage or reinforcement, rental and pet restrictions.
  8. Milestone and SIRS items (s. 553.899): anything in agendas or minutes; engineer budgeted, hired, expected completion date; fee increases or special assessments passed or discussed to pay for them.
  9. FIRPTA.
  10. Additional comments.

  No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** Seller must notify Buyer promptly in writing of changes (SPDU-1).
- **Cancel Rights:** none from the form. Buyer's condo rights are in rider A (s. 718.503 rescission, document and structural-report windows); see RCD-8.
- **Money Effects:** none directly; items 7(g) and 8(d) point to fee increases and special assessments the buyer may inherit.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none.
- **Red Flags:** any yes in 7(e) conversion or 7(f) investor purchases (termination-of-condominium risk; refer to counsel); 7(k) and 7(l) structural damage or reinforcement; 8(d) an assessment discussed but not adopted (not in the estoppel yet); 4(a) no sprinklers and a vote to forego retrofit; unapproved alterations; Don't Know across item 8 (ask for MISIRS-2 and minutes).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline has no dates. The seller's review lists association risks that can shake the buyer during the rider A windows. The offer builder budgets for fee increases and pending assessments and asks for MISIRS-2, minutes and the SIRS.

### SPDU-1 Seller's Property Disclosure Update

**Form:** `SPDU`, SPDU-1 2/20. **Used With:** any contract, after SPDR-4x or SPDC-2.

- **Purpose:** corrects or adds to a disclosure that became inaccurate.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** property and free-text corrections; seller signs, buyer acknowledges. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** "promptly" when information changes (SPDR-4x and SPDC-2 signature paragraphs). None fixed.

### FD-2 Flood Disclosure

**Form:** `FD`, FD-2 Rev 10/25. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts. Required by s. 689.302 for residential sales and **not printed in either contract**, so it is a separate form.

- **Purpose:** the statutory flood disclosure, given at or before the time the contract is executed.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** Seller name; property; (1) has / has no knowledge of flooding that damaged the property during Seller's ownership; (2) has / has not filed a flood insurance claim (including NFIP); (3) has / has not received flood-damage assistance (including FEMA); date and method a copy was provided to Buyer (email, fax, mail, personal delivery). ### SD-2 Sinkhole Disclosure

**Form:** `SD`, SD-2 Rev 2/20. **Used With:** any contract.

- **Purpose:** the seller's sinkhole disclosure: when a seller's sinkhole claim was paid, the buyer must be told before closing that it was paid and whether all proceeds went to repairs (the statutory rule SPDR-4x cites as s. 627.7073(2)(c)).

### MISIRS-2 Milestone Inspection and Structural Integrity Reserve Study Disclosure

**Form:** `MISIRS`, MISIRS-2 Rev. 1/26. **Used With:** any contract, for condos and co-ops; usually with SPDC-2. It is not a contract and doesn't replace the statutory language in rider A (condo) or COOP-4 H(10) (co-op).

- **Blanks and Defaults:**

(if yes, skip to SIRS); the last date to perform it; Phase 1 completed; Phase 2 required, completed, scheduled and when; special assessments approved (up to 4, amount and date); if none, assessments discussed at board meetings in the past 12 months; where to request those minutes.
  - **SIRS:** exempt?; completed; scheduled; SIRS-related special assessments approved (up to 4); discussed in the past 12 months; minutes.

  No defaults. Quirk: the SIRS exemption line says "If Yes, skip to questions 6 and 7", but 6 is written as a follow-up to a No on question 4. Read 6 and 7 as applying to exempt buildings too.

- **Skill Use:** the timeline has no contract dates here, but notes the milestone deadline and any assessment due dates that fall before or after closing. The seller's review flags structural-assessment risk as a certainty item during the rider A or COOP-4 windows. The offer builder adds approved assessments to the buyer's cost, asks who pays (rider A or COOP-4 H(6)), and requests the minutes.

## Condo, Cooperative and HOA Documents

### RCD-8 Receipt of Condominium/Cooperative Documents

**Form:** `RCD`, RCD-8 Rev 1/26. **Used With:** both FAR/BAR contracts, with rider A (condo) or COOP-4 (co-op).

- **Purpose:** the buyer's dated receipt for each document that starts a statutory window.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** check each that applies, each with a received date:
  - **Condominium Documents:** declaration, articles, bylaws, rules, most recent annual financial statement and budget, and the FAQ document if requested in writing.
  - **Condominium Governance Form** (DBPR).
  - **Cooperative Documents:** articles, bylaws, rules, and the question and answer sheet if requested in writing.
  - **Inspector-Prepared Milestone Inspection Summary** (condo or co-op name).
  - **Turnover Inspection Report** (for inspections on or after July 1, 2023), or a statement that none was completed.
  - **SIRS**, or a statement that none was completed.

  No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** the receipt dates **start the rescission windows**:
  - **Condo:** rider A's s. 718.503 voidability runs 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, after the later of Buyer's signing and receipt of the documents. Rider A's separate windows run from receipt of the requested documents and of the milestone summary, turnover report and SIRS.
  - **Cooperative:** COOP-4 H(9) and H(10)(d)(ii) run the same 7 business days from receipt.

  In each case Buyer may extend closing up to 7 business days after receipt, and the rights end at closing. Documents received more than 7 business days before Buyer signed support the "acknowledges receipt" option (no rescission window).
- **Cancel Rights:** none of its own; it fixes when rider A and COOP-4 rights begin and end.
- **Money Effects:** none; under rider A 6(a) and COOP-4 H(1), documents not returned after a failed deal may cost the buyer.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none; it is evidence of delivery.
- **Red Flags:** a partial set: safe reading is that a window starts only when the last document it requires is received, so use the latest date among those items; undated lines; the governance form missing for a condo; a "statement not completed" for SIRS or turnover report that the seller hasn't backed up with MISIRS-2; a buyer signature dated before the documents actually arrived.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline uses the latest applicable receipt date for each window, counts business days, and marks the deal not firm until each window ends. The seller's review urges early delivery so windows close sooner. The offer builder records the dates and warns the buyer that each right ends 7 business days after receipt.

### NTA-2 Notice to Association (Sale)

**Form:** `NTA`, NTA-2 3/00. **Used With:** any sale or lease of a condo, co-op or HOA property. Not a contract document.

- **Purpose:** the owner authorizes the licensee to receive association documents and information; the association fills in facts and returns it.
- **Blanks and Defaults:**
  - **Section 1 (Owner):** authorize the licensee to receive complete documents (including master association documents and the question and answer sheet), the budget and year-end financial report, to arrange painting or repairs, and other.
  - **Section 2 (Association):** units and rented units; purchaser application and fee; interview; right of first refusal; pet rules; vehicle and parking rules; rental restrictions and tenant application fee; whether it is a 55-and-over or 62-and-over community; RV, boat and dock facilities; unit and master association fees and how often they're paid; recreation or land lease; pending assessments and payments made; whether assessments are current and any balance owed; club and recreation costs.
  - The association is asked to attach the question and answer sheet (s. 718.504).

  No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** reports fees, pending assessments and balances owed (inputs for proration and the buyer's payment).
- **Changes to the Contract:** None.
- **Red Flags:** pending assessments or an unpaid balance; a right of first refusal (COOP-4 H(3), rider A); an application or interview requirement (approval timing); rental restrictions for an investor buyer; an old or unsigned response.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds nothing, but approval and first-refusal answers inform how early to start the association process. The seller's review uses fees and assessments in the net and approval requirements in certainty. The offer builder uses fees and assessments in the payment and restrictions as property facts. State age-restricted status and rules as facts about the property, never who they suit.

## Multiple Offers

### MODS-1 Multiple Offer Disclosure to Seller

**Form:** `MODS`, MODS-1 Rev. 5/18. **Used With:** listing side, any contract. Informational.

- **Purpose:** explains the seller's options when several offers arrive: (1) accept or counter the best and reject the rest; (2) counter some or all, reserving the right to be bound by only one accepted counter and telling each buyer it's one of several; (3) ask all for "highest and best" by a deadline. The seller decides, including whether to disclose that multiple offers exist.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** brokerage, seller, property; seller acknowledges receipt. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** None.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. Option 2 needs a written reservation in each counter; CO-3 prints none, so add it on CO-3's Other lines or an ACSP-4 addendum.
- **Red Flags:** multiple counters without the reservation (risk of two binding contracts); disclosing offers without the seller's direction.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline ignores it. The seller's review presents these three paths in its plan and notes the reservation language for option 2. The offer builder isn't affected.

### NMOB-1 Notice of Multiple Offers to Buyers

**Form:** `NMOB`, NMOB-1 Rev. 5/18. **Used With:** any contract.

- **Purpose:** tells a buyer that multiple offers exist and asks for "highest and best" by a deadline, on stated terms.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** property, MLS number, seller, listing brokerage and agent and email; buyer, selling brokerage and agent and email; Deadline (time, a.m. or p.m., date); item 7 other terms. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** by the Deadline: buyers submit written offers to the listing agent (email or personal delivery). If missed: the form is silent; Seller keeps full discretion anyway.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** None.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. Printed terms: offers in writing; Seller may accept, counter or reject any or all; Seller may negotiate further with anyone and need not talk to every buyer; price is one factor of many; if an accepted deal fails, Seller may reopen negotiations or seek new offers.
- **Red Flags:** a deadline shorter than the buyer's lender or pre-approval update needs; item 7 terms that change the process.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline ignores it. The seller's review records the deadline (`highest_and_best_due`), shows it, holds every response until it passes, scores all offers after it on the same basis, and doesn't offer another call. The offer builder treats this as the last round: best terms inside the buyer's limits, with the escalation (EAC-1) and appraisal gap (AGA-1) choices explicit, and a Para. 3 acceptance deadline that covers the seller's review time.

## Closing, Escrow and Wire Safety

### TRID-1 Authorization to Furnish TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosures

**Form:** `TRID`, TRID-1 Rev 5/16. **Used With:** any contract.

- **Purpose:** a buyer or seller authorizes the lender, title company and escrow agent to give that party's broker copies of all closing disclosures and settlement statements.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** property; Seller or Buyer (check); broker's name, license, address, phone, email; authorized associate and license (if any). No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** None.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. On a financed Standard or AS IS deal, Para. 8(b)(ii) already authorizes release of the buyer's closing disclosures to Seller and Broker; TRID-1 makes the authorization explicit for either side's own broker.
- **Red Flags:** handle the documents as nonpublic personal information and dispose of them properly (the form's note).
- **Skill Use:** the timeline may add a "closing disclosure received" check before closing. The seller's review and offer builder don't use it.

### EDRV-1 Escrow Deposit Receipt Verification

**Form:** `EDRV`, EDRV-1 4 /09. **Used With:** any contract where a title company or attorney holds the deposit.

- **Purpose:** complies with FREC Rule 61J2-14.008(2)(b): the broker who prepared or presented the offer asks the escrow agent to verify receipt of each deposit, then reports to the listing broker (or seller).
- **Blanks and Defaults:** three parts. Request: from the selling broker, to the escrow agent, parties, initial and additional deposit amounts with request dates. Verification: amounts and dates received, signed by the escrow agent. Notification to the listing broker: verification attached, or none received. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** escrow agent: written verification within **5 business days** of the request. Selling broker: copy (or notice that none came) to the listing broker or seller within **10 business days** of the request. The deposits themselves are due per Para. 2(a) (initial: with the offer, or within 3 days after Effective Date if blank) and Para. 2(b) (additional: 10 days after Effective Date if blank).
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** confirms the deposit exists; a deposit not delivered or not collected is a buyer default issue under the contract (Para. 13: failure to collect doesn't excuse Buyer).
- **Changes to the Contract:** None.
- **Red Flags:** no verification within 5 business days; amounts or dates that don't match Para. 2; an additional deposit never requested.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds each deposit due date, the request date, the 5-business-day verification and the 10-business-day notice. The seller's review asks for verification before treating the deal as firm. The offer builder plans deposit timing the buyer can meet.

### WFPN-3 Wire Fraud Prevention Notice

**Form:** `WFPN`, WFPN-3 Rev 2/20. **Used With:** any contract. Informational.

- **Purpose:** warns that emailed wiring instructions can be forged. Before wiring, call the recipient at an independently obtained number to confirm the routing (ABA or SWIFT) and account numbers. Keep sensitive data out of email.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** brokerage; buyer or seller signatures.
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** protects the balance to close and the deposit wires.
- **Changes to the Contract:** None.
- **Red Flags:** any "changed wiring instructions" email; a phone number taken from the email itself.
- **Skill Use:** the timeline adds a wire-verification reminder before deposit and closing wires. The other skills include the one-line warning in client-facing closing notes.

## Worksheets and Unfamiliar Forms

### PTS-1 Proposed Terms Sheet

**Form:** `PTS`, © 2014 (PTS-1). **Used With:** any residential contract. Not an offer or a contract; it binds no one.

- **Purpose:** a worksheet of the essential terms to fill a contract.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** seller, buyer, address, legal description, included and excluded items, price, escrow agent; initial deposit that accompanies the offer or is due within N days after Effective Date; additional deposit and days; closing date. Financing: cash, new financing ($ or %), or seller financing ($ or %). Inspection (check one): accept "as is" if the inspection is acceptable, or Seller may have to repair up to negotiated limits. Seller's and buyer's closing-cost exceptions. Title (Para. 9(c)): Seller chooses the agent and pays the owner's policy, or Buyer chooses and pays, or Buyer chooses and Seller pays, or the Miami-Dade/Broward regional provision. Special assessment installments (Para. 9(f)): split before and after closing, or Seller pays in full. Additional terms. No defaults printed (the contract's defaults apply once transferred).
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** none until transferred into a signed contract.
- **Changes to the Contract:** none. The inspection choice maps to the contract form: "as is" is the AS IS contract; "repairs up to limits" is the Standard contract with Para. 9(a) limits. Record that choice as the contract form and the scripts apply its rules; if neither is checked, ask.
- **Red Flags:** treating it as an accepted offer; no inspection choice; the regional title option chosen outside Miami-Dade or Broward.
- **Skill Use:** the offer builder may take a PTS-1 as input and map each field to the contract. The seller's review may use it for a verbal or early offer, labeled non-binding. The timeline never uses it for dates.

### FND-1 Form Notice and Disclosure

**Form:** `FND`, FND-1 Rev 7/18. **Used With:** any deal that uses a contract or addendum the agent doesn't normally use.

- **Purpose:** the brokerage tells the buyer or seller to verify property conditions and facts with specialized professionals (an attorney, a tax professional) because the form in use came from a builder or developer, an REO seller, the seller's attorney, the buyer's attorney, a lender, or other.
- **Blanks and Defaults:** brokerage; Buyer or Seller; address; the form's source (check one); signatures. No defaults.
- **Deadlines:** None.
- **Cancel Rights:** None.
- **Money Effects:** None.
- **Changes to the Contract:** None.
- **Red Flags:** its presence means the governing contract may not be FAR/BAR.
- **Skill Use:** all three skills record the contract as another contract (not FAR/BAR), work from that contract's own dates and terms, apply none of the FAR/BAR defaults in this file, and say so.

## Not in This Set

These Florida Realtors forms are intentionally not covered; name them and say where they fit when one appears:

- **CARB-1** (Compensation/Concession Addendum Related to Buyer's Broker): made for the CRSP, Vacant Land and Commercial contracts. On FAR/BAR, riders FF (seller credit related to buyer's broker compensation) and GG (compensation agreement contingency, same text as BBCCA-1) cover the same ground.
- **MDSTA-1** (Miami-Dade County Special Taxing District Addendum): for the CRSP and Vacant Land contracts. On FAR/BAR, rider CC is the Miami-Dade special taxing district disclosure.
- **QIC-1** (Qualified Improvement Addendum to Contract): for a non-ad valorem qualifying improvement assessment with an unpaid balance. On FAR/BAR, rider EE (Qualifying Improvements Disclosure, formerly PACE) covers it.
- **CFBA-1** (Conveyances to Foreign Buyers Addendum): only for contracts without the preprinted Chapter 692 language. Both FAR/BAR contracts print it, so it isn't needed.
- **ND-3** (Nonlawyer Disclosure): goes with the landlord and tenant forms, not a purchase.
- **Handouts With No Form Number:**
  - **DBPR Condominium Q&A Sheet:** the question and answer sheet that condo buyers are entitled to (rider A; RCD-8 condo documents).
  - **DBPR Cooperative Q&A Sheet:** the question and answer sheet that co-op buyers are entitled to (COOP-4 H(9); RCD-8 co-op documents).
  - **DBPR Condo Governance Form:** delivered to condo buyers (rider A 6(a), s. 718.503(2)(b)); its receipt is a line on RCD-8.
  - **Energy Efficiency Brochure:** the s. 553.996 brochure whose receipt both contracts acknowledge in Para. 10(e).
  - **Lead Info Pamphlet:** the EPA "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home" pamphlet given with rider P on pre-1978 housing (LBPL-1x 1.B(1)).
