# Condos

Read this when the property is a condo (`property_type: condo`). A condo is valued, financed and contracted differently from a house: the building and the association matter as much as the unit. Townhouses and villas with a fee-simple lot follow the house rules plus the HOA questions below.

- Adjusting

## Choosing Comps

1. **Same building first,** then the same complex, then a similar building nearby (age, height, amenities, fee level). Name which tier each comp is from.
2. Same bedroom count and within about 15% of the size. Closed within about 6 months; in a building with few sales, go back 12 months and adjust for time.
3. Skip house rules that don't apply: no pool, lot or garage adjustments unless the unit has its own (a private garage, a deeded second space).
4. A sale in a building under a special assessment or with open repairs isn't comparable to one without, until it's adjusted.

## Adjusting

List each adjustment in the data file like any other, with a sentence and a dollar amount. Typical condo adjustments (use the agent's local numbers when they have them, and say when you're estimating):

- **Floor and view:** higher floors and water or skyline views sell for more; the step per floor is local, so read it from same-building sales.
- **Parking:** a deeded or assigned space, a garage space or a second space.
- **Special assessments:** a sale where the seller paid off an assessment vs. one where the buyer takes it on. Adjust by the unpaid amount the buyer assumes.
- **Association fee:** a much higher monthly fee lowers what buyers pay for the unit. Note the gap; adjust only when the comps show it.
- **Condition** of the unit, judged from remarks as usual.

## Questions for the Seller or Listing Agent

Ask in one message, and list what's missing under the report's requests:

1. Monthly fee, what it covers (water, cable, insurance, reserves) and any increase adopted for next year.
2. Special assessments: adopted, proposed or expected, with amounts and due dates.
3. Milestone inspection status (buildings 3 stories or more, in Florida) and the structural integrity reserve study (SIRS): done, the date, and whether reserves are funded as the study requires.
4. The latest budget and financial statement, and whether the association is in litigation.
5. Lender approval: FHA-approved, VA-approved, or warrantable for conventional loans (litigation, a single owner holding many units, low owner occupancy or high commercial space can make a project non-warrantable).
6. Rental rules: minimum lease terms, waiting periods, caps. Describe the rules, never who they suit.
7. Association approval of the buyer: required or not, how long it takes, and the application fee.
8. The estoppel fee and any transfer or capital contribution fee the buyer pays.

## Lending

- An **FHA or VA** loan closes only in an approved project (or with a single-unit approval where the program allows it). Confirm it before an FHA or VA offer is written or accepted: an unapproved project can end the deal after the appraisal.
- A **non-warrantable** project needs a portfolio or non-QM loan: more down and a higher rate. Say so in the payment and the offer plan.
- The HO-6 policy covers the unit's interior; the master policy covers the building. Deductible assessments after a storm are a real cost: ask about the master policy's deductible.

## Payment

The payment includes the association fee (`hoa_monthly` or `hoa_cdd_monthly`) and, when known, a special assessment spread over its term as its own line or note. Flood insurance follows the market profile's `flood` rules: an HO-6 unit policy is exempt from Florida's Citizens flood requirement, but the building still needs flood coverage in zones A and V.

## Contract Rights

The buyer's condo rights come from the market profile's `condo` section (Florida: s. 718.503 rescission, a separate right after the milestone and SIRS summaries, association approval before closing). They are buyer rights:

- **Seller side:** deliver the association documents, the milestone summary and the SIRS with the offer or right after acceptance, because each clock starts when the buyer receives them. A deal isn't firm until those windows pass.
- **Buyer side:** note the dates the documents arrive; the rights end at closing.
- Attach the condo rider (FR/BAR CR-7x in Florida) and start the association application within the contract's deadline.

Outside a market whose profile has a `condo` section, don't assume Florida's periods: ask the agent which rider and statute apply.
