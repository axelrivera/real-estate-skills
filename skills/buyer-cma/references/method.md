# Method: History, Comps, Adjustments, Chart

## The Subject

Pull the ten facts for the fact grid (see `report-data.md`) from the property report, using the county's figures where the MLS and public records disagree (`listing-sheet.md`). Note anything unusual about the sale: an As-Is sale the public remarks state, "may be temporarily off market". Who owns or lives in the home (vacant, a tenant, a trust, estate or LLC owner) and why it's for sale stay with the agent: a watch item in the chat reply, never a report field (compute.py stops on a judgment field that describes the people). What only Realtor Information, Realtor Remarks or private notes say (an As-Is preference, the listing agent is related to the owner, proof of funds required, showing instructions) stays with the agent: never in the report, but a watch item for the agent in the chat reply (`listing-sheet.md`, What Stays with the Agent).

## The Listing History

The MLS history grid lists every change across MLS numbers, newest first: read it bottom to top. For the 360 property view's grid (status moves like `ACT->PND`, price moves like `895000.00->839000`) and checking closings against the public-record sale history, see `listing-sheet.md`. The status codes and what they mean are in the MLS layer (`mls_format.history_codes`; for Stellar: NEW, DECR/INCR, TOM/BOM, PNC, SLD, CANC/EXP/WDN).

- A new MLS number resets days on market. Look for older numbers below it and report the true timeline: first list date, total active days across all listings, every price change. Enter every row as `history.events` (`report-data.md`): compute.py counts the cuts, increases, failed contracts and active days, and the report states them itself, never a hand count.
- A row out of date order in the grid, or an MLS number that isn't the listing's (in the history or the export), is warned. Don't guess which date or listing is right: ask the agent in the reply, and count it as compute.py did until they answer.
- A pending followed by anything other than a sale means a contract failed. That's a question for the listing agent, not an assumption about the house.
- Repeated off/back-on-market pairs usually mean a seller managing showings or pausing to reset. A row that sums up undated pairs ("off and on twice") is entered as `report-data.md` describes (Undated off/on pairs).
- A price increase after a failed contract is a signal worth naming.

A relist at a higher price than a listing that failed (canceled, expired or withdrawn) is a finding too: the market already passed at the lower price.

Search the address: earlier syndicated remarks sometimes claim things (a "brand-new roof") that later vanish from the listing. That's a watch item.

## Choosing Comps

For a condo, choose and adjust comps by `condo.md` instead of the rules below.

From `stats.py`'s `sold_candidates`, pick the closest 5 sales when 5 or more qualify (4 or 6 only with a reason in `method_note`, such as a sixth sale in the subject's own subdivision, set in `comps.count`; 3 only when no more qualify: compute.py warns below 3), plus the next two or three that qualify as backups (Outliers, below), taking them in the ranking's order and skipping only the ones the rules below rule out, so the same export gives the same comps and the same median:

- same subdivision first, then an immediately comparable neighborhood within about a mile;
- within about 20% of the subject's size, same pool status, similar age and construction, closed within about 6 months.

Include the sales that argue for a higher value as well as a lower one. The buyer will find them anyway, and a report that hides them loses its credibility.

Each candidate carries `flags`: `distressed` (REO, short sale, auction) and `new_construction`. Leave those out unless the market is mostly distressed or new construction (or the subject is), then adjust for it and explain why in `method_note`. The export holds the property types the agent chose to compare. Single-family homes and townhouses can be compared when they overlap in size and price, so neither is dropped: the ranking puts the subject's type first, then close types, and puts condos, 55+ communities, leased land, a different waterfront status or a different number of stories lower. `sold_by_type` shows the mix; when the comps span types, say so in `method_note`. Duplicate sale records are already dropped (see `market_notes`), and a relisted home shows once in the competition with `listings` and `earlier_prices`. The ranking already favors recent, close sales; `more_candidates` counts the ones not listed (re-run stats.py with `--limit 30` to see them). Pass `--as-of` with the date the export was pulled so months of supply runs to that day.

## Adjusting

Give the home and each comp a condition level from the condition ladder (`condition-ladder.md`), from the listing's own words; compute.py adjusts each comp by the difference between the levels, and a condition amount typed on a card stops it. Say that the condition levels are judgment calls based on listing text.

Default rates come from the built-in market (`cma.adjustments`; built in for Florida: size at about $75/sq ft (the script's, from square footage: Size, below), $25,000 for a private pool, condition by the condition ladder's levels (`condition-ladder.md`: $15,000 for an updated kitchen, $45,000 for a full renovation, over an original home; the script takes the difference), the roof by its age on the as-of date (the script's, from roof years: Roof, below), –$5,000 for other documented recent systems (AC, water heater) the subject can't match (never for the roof as well), a better lot or water by its type (the script's: Lot, below), 1–2% per quarter when the market has shifted, set as below, and 0 for sales in about the last 6 weeks). Outside the built-in market, derive the rates from paired sales in the export, scaled to the price, and say so. Explain any departure in `method_note`.

The built-in rates are flat dollars from Central Florida sales in one price band (`cma.calibrated_for`). compute.py warns when the home is outside that area or band: then derive the rates from paired sales in the export, or use the agent's, and scale flat amounts (a pool, a renovation) to the price. There are no built-in rates for garage spaces, bedroom or bath count, age, or size differences over about 300 sq ft: derive those from paired sales and say so, or leave the difference to the range and explain it.

- Subtract seller-paid buyer costs from the sale price, dollar for dollar.
- **Size.** The script's: the difference between this home's `sqft` and each comp's living area in the export (or the card's `sqft` when the export doesn't have the sale), times the market's rate per square foot (built in for Florida: $75), to the dollar. Never type a size amount (compute.py stops on one). The rate is for differences under about 300 sq ft: compute.py warns on a comp farther off (replace it with the next qualifying sale, or keep it and say why in `method_note`). Outside the built-in market, give `comps.size_per_sqft` from paired sales. A bedroom, bath or story difference stays a typed adjustment from paired sales.
- **Lot.** The script's, by lot type. Give this home and each comp a `lot` from its listing's own words: `standard` (when left out), `corner_or_oversized` (a corner lot, or one noticeably larger than the street's), `view` (a pond, preserve, conservation or golf view), `canal` or `lake_or_open_water`. Built in for Florida: corner or oversized and view $5,000, canal and lake or open water $10,000 over a standard lot; compute.py adjusts each comp by the difference between its lot's value and this home's. A lot the types don't cover (a busy road, an unusually small lot) is `other`, with its `lot_value` (dollars over a standard lot, negative when worth less, from paired sales or the agent) and `lot_reason` in words. Never type a lot or water amount (compute.py stops on one).
- **Roof.** The script's, by the market's roof-age bands (built in for Florida: shingle under 10 years $0, 10–14 years –$5,000, 15–19 years –$10,000, 20 or more –$15,000; double the years for tile or metal), adjusting each comp by the difference between its band and this home's (a comp with a 7-year roof against this home's 16-year roof is –$10,000). Enter `roof_year` (and `roof_type`: `tile` or `metal`, shingle when left out) for this home and for each comp whose listing or remarks give it; never type a roof amount on a card (compute.py stops on one). When this home's roof year isn't known, no comp is adjusted for its roof and the report says so: never guess it. A comp without a roof year gets no roof adjustment and compute.py warns, so look for it in the remarks.
- **Time (market shift).** The script's, by one rule, so the same export always gives the same rate. The market split is the first of the month two months before `as_of` (a report as of September 26 counts sales from July 1 as recent). The rate is the change in median sale-to-original-list from the earlier sales to the recent ones: under 1 point, none; 1 to 3 points, 1% a quarter; over 3, 2% a quarter, added when the ratio rose and taken off when it fell; none when either side has fewer than 5 sales. compute.py gives every comp that closed before the split the rate times the quarters from its close date to `as_of`, on the sale price minus seller-paid costs, to $100, and a sale on or after the split none. Never type a split date, a rate or a time amount (compute.py stops on them). Only the agent's own choice goes in an override, with their reason in words, and the report says so: `split_override` `{date, reason}`, `comps.time_override` `{rate_per_quarter, prices, reason}` (`0` for none). The method line names the rate and the split, and each card shows its own time amount: never restate them. The gut check uses the same rule.
- List each comp's adjustments in the report data (`sold_price`, `seller_concessions`, `adjustments`); compute.py does the arithmetic and fills the card and the summary table from the same numbers. More than about 15% net or 25% gross of the sale price (common appraisal guidelines) means a weak comp: replace it, or explain why it stays.
- **Outliers.** The script's, by one rule. Write the cards in the ranking's order: the closest five (`comps.count` when the method above allows another number), then the next two or three qualifying candidates as backups. compute.py uses the first five; a used comp more than 10% above or below the median of the other used comps is set aside, the farthest first, and the next backup takes its place; it repeats until none is flagged (with no backup left, it sets one aside only while three remain). The report names each sale set aside. Never drop or reorder a card to move the range. A flagged sale stays only on the agent's call: `keep_reason` on its card (words, shown in the report). When compute.py warns that an outlier is left with no backup, add the next candidates as backups and re-run.
- Each card prints its adjustments with their amounts; its `bullets` say in words why ("It sold earlier in the year, when rates were lower and homes were moving faster"), without the figures.

## The Range

The supported range is the script's, by one rule, so the same comps always give the same range: the normal width (the widest $5,000 step under about 6% of the median adjusted value: $25,000 at $440,000, $20,000 at $386,800) centered on the median adjusted value, the low end rounded half up to $5,000 and the high end the low end plus the width, pulled in when the rounding would put an end past what the comps support (one comp never sets an end: an end stays at or inside the second-highest or second-lowest adjusted value, or half the normal width from the median, whichever reaches farther). compute.py prints it at the comps stage (report.json with the subject and the comps only) and uses it everywhere after: never type the range. The way to move it is the comps: replace an outlier or the weakest match (the largest adjustments, the farthest or oldest sale) and re-run. Say in `comps.lean` (judgment, no figures) which sales the evidence leans on.

Only when the agent chose a range themselves, give `range_override` `{low, high, reason}`: `reason` is judgment, figure-free, and the report shows the range as the agent's, beside the method's. compute.py still warns when an override is wider than the cap (`range_wide`), narrower than half of it (`range_narrow`) or set by one comp (`range_one_comp`), naming the method's range.

## The Scatterplot

The script marks the comp cards' sales as Comparable Sales (matched to the export by street address, case aside, so write each card's street address as the export spells it: "436 SUMMIT DR" or "436 Summit Dr", never "436 Summit Drive"; the report prints an all-caps address in title case), draws the other sales and the listings, computes the size-only trend line and notes how many homes were left off the chart for size. Pick 1–3 callouts, usually the top-selling comp and the strongest active competitor, each a home the chart plots (a sale or an active listing; never a pending one, and render.py names any callout that isn't on the chart: drop it or pick a plotted home). Each label goes on its `side` unless it would cover a marker or another label; then the script moves it and says so, and names any label it couldn't clear.
