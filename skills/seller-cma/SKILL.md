# Seller CMA

A report (and, when the agent wants one, a listing presentation) for a homeowner deciding what to list at. It answers three questions: what is the home worth, what should we list it at, and what will I walk away with. It works because it's honest: a seller who sees only flattering comps overprices, loses the first weeks, and blames the agent. The scripts do the math, the charts, the layout and the deck. Your part is the intake, the comp judgment, and plain words for a homeowner who isn't a real estate professional.

Every number comes from a script, never typed by hand, because a wrong net figure in front of a seller is the worst failure this skill can produce. Never invent comps, update dates, permit status or tax figures.

## Guardrails

These apply to everything this skill writes: files, chat replies, and text the agent may forward to a client.

- **Fair housing.** Describe the property, the numbers and the terms, never people: not who the home suits, who should buy, or who lives nearby. No claims about safety, crime, school quality or who makes up an area. The protected classes are race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status and national origin, plus sexual orientation, gender identity and any listed in the market's `fair_housing.extra_protected_classes`. Read `references/fair-housing.md` before writing findings, value drivers, the launch plan or deck notes. Value drivers are features of the home and the launch plan is about the property and the process, never a kind of buyer. If the agent asks for wording that breaks this, write the compliant version and say why in one sentence; don't lecture or flag innocent wording like "family room".
- **No em dashes in prose,** chat included: use a comma, colon, parentheses or a new sentence. A lone em dash for an empty value (a table cell with nothing in it) is fine.
- **Labels in Title Case:** headings, column headers, row names, tiles, legend entries, card and slide titles. Sentences, notes and table values stay sentence case.
- **`render.py` checks the data file first** (and any deck, CMA or profile file it reads) and stops on an em dash in a sentence, a clear fair-housing red flag, or tool words, data keys, ISO dates or jargon in client text (`references/client-wording.md`), listing every field at once. Rewrite each field; don't work around the check. Labels are put in Title Case for you. It can't see chat replies, so the rules above still apply there.

## 1. Gather the Inputs

Ask for everything missing in one message, and skip what's already in the chat or the listing. Don't ask about local costs or commission before building: they're defaults the agent can correct after the first report (a reprice's reply asks for the listing agreement's commission, below). Read `references/method.md` for the full checklist and why each item matters.

The agent usually uploads the home's **MLS property report** (Stellar's Cross Property 360 PDF). It answers the fact questions (county record of size, rooms, lot, year built, construction, pool, garage, HOA, taxes, flood zone) and holds the full listing history. It's often from the seller's purchase or an old listing, so its remarks, photos and condition are history, not today's home: ask the seller what changed since. Read `references/listing-sheet.md` for how to read it and what never goes in a seller-facing file. Without it, ask for the facts below. In short:

- **From the seller:** address; beds, baths, heated sq ft, lot, year built, construction; pool, garage, HOA/CDD; updates with dates and permits (roof first); the current tax bill; known issues or claims; timeline and occupancy; optional mortgage payoff (turns the net sheet into cash at closing).
- **From the agent:** the MLS CMA export (CSV) of nearby sales from about the last 6 months plus active, pending, expired and canceled listings; the brokerage terms if they volunteer them (otherwise 5% total is the default, shown with no label and named in the reply); the tax bill and expected closing date for the proration; the property type (Miami-Dade surtax); flood zone if known.

The scripts drop every export row with the home's address, and its facts come from the property report and the seller. stats.py lists those rows in `subject_rows`, and the property report's status line and history show the same. An expired, withdrawn or canceled listing in the history is a failed price, however long ago: copy each one into report.json's `listing_history` (status, last price, first price, end date, days on market, from the property report; the export's own rows fill it when you leave it out), and page 1, the deck and the reply name it (compute.py's `history_line`). Never guess whether it came before the seller's updates; when its dates aren't known, ask for them in the reply. When it ended within the last 12 months (or its dates aren't known), that makes this a **relist**: copy stats.py's `relist` into report.json, and compute.py caps every option at the failed price unless the agent gives a reason to go higher (`relist.reason_above`; `method.md`, A Relist). A listing that ended more than 12 months ago is history only: name it, but the market has moved since, so it sets no cap.

**Listed right now** (stats.py's `listed_now`, or an active or pending status on the property report): stop before any pricing. Tell the agent the home shows as active or pending, at what price and for how long, and ask which applies. Build nothing until the agent answers, and ask only this: save the other intake questions for after the answer (they're wasted if it's another brokerage's live listing). When the agent already said which, don't ask again: pass the matching flag to stats.py.

- **The agent's own listing** (`--own-listing`): build it as a reprice. When it shows as pending, first ask whether that contract is still alive: a price change applies only once the sale has fallen through and the home is back on the market. Copy stats.py's `reprice` into report.json (`current_price`, `days_on_market`, and `original_price` when the listing started higher); the report names the listing's price history (compute.py's `price_history`), and reads "New List Price" and "Before We Reprice". compute.py builds "Stay at Current Price" first (its expected sale by the rule in `method.md`), then price cuts only, never an increase unless the agent asks for one (`reprice.allow_increase`). The listing agreement already sets the commission: when the agent hasn't given it, the nets assume 5% (labeled), and the reply asks for the agreement's terms.
- **A listing that has ended or is ending** (a pending sale that fell through, a listing about to expire or be withdrawn), when the agent says so: offer two ways and let the agent pick. A sale that fell through for financing or inspection often wasn't turned down on price: say so, since public record may fit better then. **As a failed listing** (`--listed-as failed`): its price caps the options like an expired one; copy stats.py's `relist` to report.json (`method.md`, A Relist). **As public record** (`--listed-as history`): name it in the report as history; it sets no cap.
- **Another brokerage's listing that's still live:** don't build it. The agent must not solicit a home listed with someone else; offer to run it when that listing ends.

For a PDF or deck, the agent's name and brokerage go on it: use their profile (found as `references/saved-files.md` describes), or ask for the two in the same message. If there's no answer, build without them (the files leave them out, never a placeholder) and say so in the reply; render.py prints the reminder.

**No MLS export** (the agent typed a few comps): skip stats.py, write the comps and competition from what you were given, and name them in `sources`; there's no market table without an export (the comps carry the numbers). Never invent an earlier period: page 1 and the deck's market cards fall back to the comps on their own. The scatter chart (report and slide) is left out on its own; say so in the reply, in one line, with why (no export to plot). For adjustment rates without paired sales, see `references/method.md`.

**Location.** The city and county set the costs, taxes and MLS. With only a street address, take them from stats.py's `subject_location` (the export's own row for the home) and say so; if it has none, ask. Never infer them from subdivision names, or the state from the MLS: build with national estimates (compute.py marks the report Preliminary), and quote compute.py's `state_unknown` line (in `assumptions`) in the reply, which says what the MLS's state would change ("if this is Florida, the net sheet uses the 0.70% documentary stamp tax instead of the 0.4% estimate").

Local costs come from the home's location: read `references/local-costs.md`, including its one rule for the property tax at closing (a closing after bills go out assumes this year's bill unpaid unless the agent says it's paid). Florida and Stellar MLS are built in; elsewhere, national estimates, named once in the net sheet's notes (never Florida's numbers), and you look up the state's transfer tax from a trusted source.

## 2. Read the Market

```
python3 scripts/stats.py export.csv --address "<address as in the export>" --sqft <sqft> [--pool] --subdivision "<name>" [--type <property_type>] [--lat <lat> --lon <lon>] --state <ST> --county <county> [--mls <MLS>] [--columns columns.json] [--split-date YYYY-MM-DD] [--as-of YYYY-MM-DD] [--own-listing | --listed-as failed|history]
```

When the agent states today's date ("Today is September 26"), use it, never the system clock, and don't question it: it's report.json's `prepared_date` and `as_of`, and stats.py's `--as-of`. Pick a split date so "recent" is roughly the last 2–3 months. When the export has no Distance column (a zip or subdivision search), distances come from Latitude and Longitude: pass the home's `--lat`/`--lon` (and `latitude`/`longitude` in report.json) when it has no row of its own in the export. When stats.py says the export has a built-in MLS's columns (Stellar) but no MLS was given, re-run with `--mls` and the state and county. Copy stats.py's `mls` into report.json's `mls`, and its `relist` when it prints one. For an MLS that isn't built in, map the export's headers to the field names in `references/report-data.md` and pass them with `--columns` (and as `export_columns` in report.json). Search the web for the latest Freddie Mac 30-year rate, the county's current millage when there's none built in for the home's taxing district, and outside Florida the state's transfer tax. Cite them in your reply. Cite the rate as Freddie Mac's PMMS page (https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) with its week. Cite only a page you opened, never a news-release or other URL you haven't opened. Never cite data dated after the report date (`as_of`): the report, the deck and the reply describe the market as of that date, so a rate or sale published later stays out, even in an offer to update.

Read `references/method.md` for choosing and adjusting comps, the range, the pricing stance and the options it sets, and `references/condition-ladder.md` for each home's condition level. For a condo, also read `references/condo.md` (comps, adjustments, association and lending questions).

## 3. Write report.json

Write it in a temporary folder, never the outputs folder (`references/saved-files.md`, Working Files). Copy `assets/example-report.json` (an approved report) and replace every value; its length and tone are the target. It describes a sample home with illustrative details: take its structure and tone, never a fact (a sale price, a repair, a record) into a real report. Read `references/report-data.md` for every field, `references/costs.md` before the costs and buyer-payment blocks, and `references/writing.md` for how each section reads. Write `summary_page` last.

report.json holds **data** (prices, dates, the comps and their adjustments, the costs) and **judgment** in your words: why this price, what it means for the seller, which way the range leans, what to prepare (`prep.items`, which page 1 and the deck's launch plan show too), the conditions behind an option. Judgment carries **no figures**: no digits, `$`, `%`, months, seasons, weekdays or `{placeholders}`, and never describes who owns or lives in the home (vacant, a tenant, relocating: those stay with the agent). The scripts write every count, price, percent, date and comparison in their own sentences beside yours: the range and the median, where the list price sits, the strongest match and the highest sale, the market's numbers, the launch date, each option's closing. A figure typed into a judgment field stops compute.py, naming each field; so does a field the script now writes (`recommendation.paragraph`, `key_stats`, `market.rows`, a card's `meta`), with where your words go instead. Write "the earlier sales", "repeated price cuts", "the four-point inspection", never "April", "the spring sales" or "4 cuts".

Only when the agent asked for the presentation (see Deliver), put its wording under `deck` in report.json (copy `assets/example-deck-content.json`; read `references/deck-content.md`). It is judgment too: every price, net, payment, date and count on a slide comes from the same computed figures as the PDF.

The range and the prices are compute.py's, by rule (`method.md`): run it first with only `subject` and `comps` (no `pricing` or `buyer_payment` yet) and it prints the range, the median adjusted value and the pricing stance the market data suggests, with the list price each stance sets. Pick the stance (`pricing.stance`: `draw_offers`, `market` or `premium`): keep the suggestion, or give `pricing.stance_reason` (judgment, no figures) for a different one. The script sets the list price and builds the options (a new listing: Draw Offers, Market Price and Premium, the stance marking the recommended one); never type a price or a range. Only an agent's own figures go in by hand: their price in `price_override`, their range in `range_override` (each with a `reason`), an option's time, seller credit or expected sale in `pricing.options`. Then compute:

```
python3 scripts/compute.py report.json
```

Each option's time, seller credit and `expected_sale` are the script's (the expected sale is the list price times the recent sale-to-final-list ratio plus its seller credit, kept inside the range; `method.md`). compute.py stops on data that's wrong, listing every problem as `field: problem → fix`: fix them all, then re-run. Fix every item in `warnings` (an agent's price outside the range, a missing tax rate) and re-run. For a chat-only answer, compute.py needs `subject`, `comps.cards`, `costs` and `buyer_payment` (`pricing.stance` when you don't take the suggestion); the prose sections and `deck` can stay short. Tell the agent about each item in `assumptions` (default brokerage, estimated costs, built-in title fees, the payoff estimate, the holding-cost interest rate, taxes without a homestead exemption). A payoff the seller states ("about $171,500 from their statement") is `costs.mortgage_payoff`, used as given; only a loan balance is `mortgage_balance` (`references/report-data.md`). It also saves `<address>.seller.cma.json` next to report.json, the handoff seller-offer-review reads later in this conversation: a working file, never shown or offered to the agent.

## 4. Deliver

- **Report (the default):**
  ```
  python3 scripts/render.py report.json [--profile profile.md]   # the PDF; the profile puts the agent's name and colors on it
  ```
  Build the listing presentation only when the agent asked for it in the request ("listing presentation", "deck", "slides", "PowerPoint"): then write `deck` and use `--format all`. A request for only the presentation still gets the report PDF too (the full analysis behind the slides, and the seller's leave-behind): deliver the report, the PPTX and the deck's PDF copy. Otherwise deliver the PDF and offer the presentation in one line; if they say yes, write `deck` and run `--format pptx` (the PDF is already made). The presentation comes as the editable PPTX plus a PDF copy of the slides (a backup that opens anywhere); deliver both. Read what it prints: page 1 must fit on one page (shorten the summary wording, never drop an element); chart labels move clear of markers on their own (a label's `side` is a preference: the line saying one moved is information), and if it says labels still cover a marker or another label, drop that callout or shorten its label; a callout it says isn't on the chart (a pending home, or one left off for size or price): drop it or pick a plotted home; a page it says is under half full, or a last page with only a few lines, means shortening the wording it names; a slide line that says text doesn't fit names the `deck` field to shorten before you rebuild. Look at every page (and slide, from the deck's PDF) before presenting; `references/deck-content.md` explains how to check the deck. If the deck can't be built (no Node), say so plainly and deliver the PDF.
- **Summary in chat:** fill in `assets/seller-cma-template.md` from compute.py's output only: every figure and fact sentence already written, your judgment beside them. Never paste JSON or code blocks into a reply: the agent isn't technical, and seller-offer-review reads the handoff file compute.py saved.

Either way, the reply holds, in this order:

1. The recommended (or new) price and range, the pricing stance by its name (`stance.name`), and the one or two facts driving it. On a reprice or relist, one of them is the listing's price history, as compute.py's `price_history` gives it.
2. How the options compare, in a line or two, each by its `label` (the same names as the files). Quote the rounded differences compute.py gives (each strategy's `net_vs_recommended_about` and `net_spread_about`), never a figure you rounded yourself. They compare nets after holding costs to each option's own closing (`net_basis`), the same nets page 1, the pricing table and the deck show.
3. What the agent can replace to sharpen the nets (brokerage terms, estimated costs, payoff, title company quote), as `references/local-costs.md` shows, and each assumption compute.py listed (an unpaid tax bill, the holding-cost interest rate).
4. Anything to confirm: what the seller still owes you (update dates, flood zone), a missing name or brokerage, the failed listing's dates on a relist, the listing agreement's commission on a reprice, questions for the seller.
5. "Preliminary" and compute.py's `preliminary_reason`, when it's marked so.
6. The other format, offered in one line.

When files are delivered, keep items 1 to 6 to short bullets under about 250 words: the files carry the detail, and a long reply repeating them gets skimmed. Outside that count: the line naming the files, the missing name and brokerage line, and the rate's source in one line. Measured, items 1 to 6 with a price history, five assumptions and three things to confirm run about 220 words, so they fit; cut explanation before any of them. A chat-only answer can run longer, since the template is the answer.

**A quick answer** ("quick one", "just tell me"): skip the template and keep to about 250 words, the table included. It holds the price, range and expected sale in one line; the options table (list price, time, Est. Net, buyer's payment, on the template's basis) with the per-$10,000 line; the comparison line; the assumptions and questions as one combined list, one short line each; the Preliminary line when marked; and the full report offered in one line. When it runs long, cut the "why" first (to one clause at most), then the explanation inside the list items; never an assumption, a question or the Preliminary line.
